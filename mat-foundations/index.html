Mat Foundation Design in Blenheim – Raft Slab Engineering for Variable Ground

Knowledgeable. Thorough. Resourceful.

Blenheim sits at the confluence of the Wairau and Opawa rivers, which means the town has grown over deep sequences of Holocene alluvium. The original colonial settlement expanded onto drained swampland, and later subdivisions pushed into the Wither Hills fan gravels. This patchwork of fluvial deposits creates abrupt changes in bearing stiffness across a single building footprint. A raft or mat foundation is often the only rational way to manage differential settlement when a site straddles old channel fills and stiffer interfluve gravels. The engineering logic is straightforward: stiffen the entire footprint so the structure moves as one rigid body. Our team applies the NZGS guideline on shallow foundation design to every Blenheim project, coupling it with CPT testing in the Wairau Plain silts and liquefaction analysis where groundwater is within 3 m of the surface. The local water table, typically 1.5 to 2.5 m below ground level in the central town area, makes buoyancy checks and drainage detailing mandatory.

On Blenheim’s river-plain soils, a properly designed raft converts unpredictable differential settlement into predictable uniform tilt – and that is a problem engineers can solve.

How we work

The Wairau Valley experiences a rain-shadow effect from the Richmond Range, producing dry summers and cool winters with frost penetration reaching 200 mm in exposed sites. This seasonal moisture fluctuation causes shrinkage and heave in the cohesive alluvial silts that underlie much of Blenheim's residential and light commercial zones. A raft foundation designed with perimeter stiffening beams and a 250–350 mm structural slab bridges these soft spots and prevents the hairline cracking that plagues lightly reinforced house slabs in the region. Where the gravels pinch out and the silt thickness exceeds 4 m, we integrate stone columns to improve subgrade modulus before the raft is cast, ensuring the overall bearing pressure stays below 75 kPa under service loads. The design process follows NZS 3404 for steel reinforcement detailing and NZS 4203 for load combinations, with subgrade reaction values derived from field plate load tests or back-calculated from CPT tip resistance correlations.

Site-specific factors

A three-storey apartment block on Grove Road was designed with isolated pad footings after a desktop study indicated dense gravel at 2 m depth. During excavation, the contractor hit saturated organic silt lenses that extended to 4.5 m, with SPT N-values of 3 to 5. The foundation design was revised to a 500 mm structural raft with thickened edges, and the slab was cast directly onto a compacted gravel working platform after removing the organic material. The alternative – deep piling through the silt – would have added six weeks and $80,000 to the programme. This is a common scenario in Blenheim’s older grid blocks between Alfred Street and the Taylor River, where buried paleochannels are invisible at the surface. Without a raft solution, differential settlement of 25–40 mm between the stiff and soft zones would have caused frame distortion and partition wall cracking within the first two years of occupation. Post-construction level surveys confirmed total settlement under 10 mm with negligible differential movement.

Quick answers

What is the typical cost range for a raft foundation design in Blenheim?

The engineering design fee – covering geotechnical investigation, structural analysis, and PS1/PS2 documentation – generally falls between NZ$1.920 and NZ$6.710, depending on the building footprint area, number of storeys, and complexity of the ground profile.

When is a raft foundation preferred over piles in the Wairau Plain?

A raft is preferred when the competent bearing stratum is deeper than 3–4 m and the overlying soil is compressible silt or loose sand. It eliminates the cost of deep piling and reduces the risk of differential settlement between pile-supported elements and ground-bearing slabs.

How do you determine the modulus of subgrade reaction for Blenheim silts?

We correlate the value from CPT cone resistance using published relationships validated for New Zealand alluvial soils, and calibrate it against in-situ plate load tests when the project budget allows. The NZGS shallow foundation guideline provides the framework for selecting design kv values.

Does the Marlborough District Council require specific raft design documentation?

Yes. A Producer Statement PS1 (design) and PS2 (construction review) are required under the Building Act. The raft design must demonstrate compliance with NZS 3404 and NZS 4203, and the geotechnical report must address liquefaction potential and bearing capacity per the NZGS module on earthquake geotechnical engineering.
